Six strategic blind spots warrant correction:

1. **HIGH — User discovery comes after substantial commitment.**  
   **Evidence:** §2, “Competitive and audience check,” places three to five task-based sessions “Before expanding P3-P5”; §4.10 allocates months 1–2 to P0/P1, local P2 and a frozen study.  
   **Already planned:** Audience validation and task observation. **Remaining gap:** Those sessions principally test the proposed workflow after its architecture has been selected; they do not establish that contract verification solves a recurring problem worth that investment.  
   **Correction:** Before committing the full first slice, observe intended users completing real tasks with their current tools. Identify one task, its failure cost and its acceptable review burden. Keep customer identity and spending capacity unknown until established.

2. **HIGH — The alternatives omit a verification-first product boundary.**  
   **Evidence:** §1.3 compares “Qualify the local core,” “Staged local core plus optional hosted profile,” and “Install the full proposed stack.” §2 proposes comparison against a stronger generator.  
   **Already planned:** Generator comparisons and reversible adapters. **Remaining gap:** All three strategic options retain ownership of an integrated generation system; the document does not test whether importing externally generated images and returning calibrated contract evidence delivers most of the value.  
   **Correction:** Add a fourth alternative: contract compiler, independent verifier and evidence export over existing generators. Compare task completion, CSR, setup burden and maintenance against the integrated local workflow. Preserve local generation where its measured contribution warrants ownership.

3. **HIGH — The usefulness thresholds have no demonstrated task basis.**  
   **Evidence:** P0 proposes “>=30% qualified yield,” “<=50% unverified/abstained,” and a “600-second total job deadline with at most two repairs.”  
   **Already planned:** Owner agreement, frozen thresholds, latency reporting and false-rejection review. **Remaining gap:** Owner approval alone cannot show that waiting up to ten minutes for this yield is useful for the eventual task.  
   **Correction:** Derive thresholds from observed workflows and report time and total cost per qualified result, including unsuccessful submissions and human inspection. For local operation, include GPU occupancy and operator attention; for any proposed hosting, separately establish spending limits.

4. **HIGH — The initial study may not support the promotion decision it is asked to make.**  
   **Evidence:** P0 specifies 120 contracts × four seeds × two profiles, a positive paired 95% CSR interval, no category degradation above five percentage points, and at least 100 accepted cases without a confirmed false accept.  
   **Already planned:** Family-clustered analysis, uncertainty reporting, a hard execution cap and experimental status for inconclusive results. **Remaining gap:** No supplied power analysis establishes whether the number of independent families, category counts or accepted outputs can resolve those gates; the exact category-regression decision rule is also unspecified.  
   **Correction:** Before freezing the study, define the minimum worthwhile CSR improvement, category inference rules and expected accepted-case yield. Calculate required independent sample sizes. If these exceed available annotation or laptop compute, explicitly scope the first study to feasibility and estimation.

5. **HIGH — Differentiation is asserted as a hypothesis without an incremental-value test.**  
   **Evidence:** §2 calls “portable contracts and calibrated evidence on constrained hardware” the defensible product hypothesis and warns that a stronger foundation model does not establish FrameGenX’s value.  
   **Already planned:** Dated competitor corrections and stronger-generator comparisons. **Remaining gap:** Generator comparisons alone cannot isolate the contribution of FrameGenX’s contract compilation, verifier, controller and repair policy.  
   **Correction:** Benchmark a current workflow, that workflow plus FrameGenX verification, and the full FrameGenX loop on matched tasks and budgets. Measure where each addition improves CSR or reduces user effort. Competitive capabilities and current implementation behavior remain **unknown** beyond the supplied statements.

6. **MEDIUM — There is no stop or pivot rule for the core product hypothesis.**  
   **Evidence:** §4.10 says an inconclusive result should lead to spending “the next cycle on diagnosed failures”; months 5–6 choose between another accuracy study and hosting.  
   **Already planned:** Budget caps, rejection of unsuccessful integrations and protection against premature hosting. **Remaining gap:** Repeated core research can continue without a stated limit, while hosting readiness could be mistaken for product progress.  
   **Correction:** Set a bounded review after a specified number of frozen studies and user observations. Continue integrated generation only with meaningful CSR improvement and repeat task use; otherwise narrow the supported domain, pivot toward verification tooling, or retain research-only status. Hosting requires demonstrated demand as well as operational readiness.
